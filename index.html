Watch a SMART Health Link get built, encrypted, and read back, live in this page

This walks through the design in MDR's pre-check-in build guide end to end: choosing what to share, assembling the package, encrypting it, and having a practice read it back. Every step below runs for real in your browser, using the same AES-256-GCM encryption and QR encoding the reference build uses. Nothing here is sent anywhere. The decryption key never leaves this page.

01

The app learns of a visit

A visit reaches an app like MDR one of five ways: read from the practice's EHR, booked in the app, found by scanning the patient's own calendar, imported from a calendar file, or typed in by hand. Here it is a FHIR Appointment read from a practice's API, for a primary care visit next Tuesday.

The Appointment resource the app just read


          

            The other four ways don't all look like this. Most patients never type
            a visit into any app at all. A confirmation text arrives, they tap "Add to Calendar," and
            it lands in their phone's own calendar, not here. Try the scan below, it runs the exact
            matching logic the reference build uses to find visits like that on its own.

03
The app builds the package

        

          

            The selected records go into a FHIR Bundle shaped to the KTC guide's PatientSharedBundle
            profile: type collection, one Patient entry, the chosen resources, and a
            rendered summary the practice can read without a FHIR viewer. The real build renders that
            summary as a PDF; here it's shown as the same text before it's laid onto a page.

04
Encrypt the bundle into a SMART Health Link

        

          

            The bundle is encrypted with a fresh 256-bit key using AES-256-GCM, exactly as the KTC guide
            specifies for a flag-U link: algorithm dir, encryption
            A256GCM, content type application/fhir+json. The key travels
            inside the link. It is never sent to a server, here or in the real build.

05
Route B: a QR code at the desk

        

          

            This is the route every practice can use with no integration at all. The patient shows this
            code, front-desk staff scan it the way they'd scan a boarding pass, and the practice's own
            EHR fetches the encrypted file. It expires in 15 minutes, like the reference build's default.
